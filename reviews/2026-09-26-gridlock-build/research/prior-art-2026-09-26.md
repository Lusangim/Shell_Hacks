## What the last 30 days showed
- Almost no public discussion of PaverOps-style tools by name; planners on r/urbanplanning still describe
  spreadsheets and whiteboards (thread: https://www.reddit.com/r/urbanplanning/comments/1wi39xj/).
- Vendors pitch automatic conflict detection with notices drafted for the owners (4M Analytics,
  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=heANDdLcr18), built around FHWA's SHRP2 R15B Utility Conflict Matrix.
- City right-of-way coordination is an established, map-first category (Coordinate / dotMaps; Chicago's
  Project Coordination Office; SEMCOG's capital-project hub).
- Planned-transmission data is consolidating but gated behind forms, PDFs or paid exports; SERTP posts plans
  as documents with no interactive map; ACEG's 2025 report card kept the Southeast at F, citing access to
  information (https://www.utilitydive.com/news/transmission-planning-development-aseg/811197/).
- GIS practitioners distrust quickly built map tools that misuse data; provenance earns credibility
  (https://www.reddit.com/r/gis/comments/1wbpqp9/).

## Pitch lines (verify "snippet" claims before use)
- Tools like PaverOps, Coordinate and Accela catch overlapping street works for cities and utilities.
  GridLock does the same for transmission plans across utility borders, using public filings only.
- London's coordination service reports 1,300+ days of disruption avoided since 2019 (snippet); Toronto
  reviews projects 3–5 years out. GridLock gives transmission planners the same early look at overlaps.
- Order 1920 asks neighbouring regions to share long-term needs and possible solutions; DESC (SCRTP) and
  the Georgia utilities (SERTP) sit on exactly that boundary. GridLock is a public, page-cited first look.
- ACEG kept the Southeast at F and named access to information; GridLock turns the region's public PDFs
  into a searchable overlap map.
- *Competitors?* Street-level: PaverOps, Coordinate, Accela, Esri, one.network — built for streets and
  shared among governments and utilities. Grid-side: data aggregators (Our Grid Future, Interconnection.fyi)
  and regional planning sites. None found pairs two utilities' public transmission plans by distance and
  timing with page citations.
- *Why doesn't this exist?* SERTP posts documents only; inputs are gated (cost estimates under
  confidentiality, models in a CEII area, a form for Our Grid Future, paid exports); the free federal line
  layer (HIFLD Open) was retired in 2025; the cross-region sharing duty (Order 1920) is new.
